# 9. Result:

The experimental observations of the Common Source (CS) amplifier show the practical trade-off between voltage gain, circuit stability, and voltage headroom. With a supply voltage of 1 V and a low threshold voltage of 0.25 V, the transistors obtain sufficient overdrive voltage. Because of this, the MOSFETs remain in the saturation region . This helps the amplifier maintain good linear behavior and prevents distortion or clipping in the output signal.

# 10. Validation:

The obtained results confirm that the amplifier operates within the expected theoretical conditions. Since the transistors stay in the saturation region throughout the signal swing, the circuit maintains stable amplification and proper biasing. The absence of clipping and the consistent output waveform verify that the design parameters, such as supply voltage and device threshold voltage, are suitable for reliable amplifier operation.

# 11. Inference:

The diode-connected load configuration reduces the overall voltage gain but improves the bandwidth and linearity of the amplifier. This approach is often used in integrated circuit design because large resistors are difficult to implement on a chip. By using an active load, the gain becomes dependent mainly on the transistor dimensions (W/L ratios) rather than precise resistance values, making the circuit performance more predictable.

# Resistive Load:
* Simple Implementation: Uses a passive resistor as the load, making the circuit easy to design and analyze.
Good Output Voltage Swing: Since only one transistor is present in the signal path, the output can vary over a wide voltage range.
* Higher Power Consumption: Large resistors may require higher voltage drops, which can increase power dissipation.
Large Chip Area: In integrated circuits, high-value resistors occupy significant silicon area.

# Cascode Amplifier:
* High Output Resistance: The additional transistor increases output resistance, leading to improved amplifier performance.
* Better Isolation: The upper transistor isolates the input from output variations, improving stability.
* Reduced Effect of Channel Length Modulation: Helps maintain a more constant drain current and improves gain accuracy.
* Higher Design Complexity: Requires additional biasing and careful design compared to a simple CS amplifier.

# Diode-Connected Load:
* Automatic Biasing: The gate and drain connection automatically sets the operating point of the transistor.
* Compact Structure: Requires less silicon area compared to large resistors in integrated circuits.
* Lower Output Resistance: The effective resistance of the diode-connected transistor is small, limiting gain.
* Common in Current Mirrors: Often used in analog circuits where active loads and current mirrors are required.
